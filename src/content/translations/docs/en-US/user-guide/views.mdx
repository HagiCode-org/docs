HagiCode shows the same sessions through **five views**. Each view answers a different question: *what is this session saying?*, *where does my work stand?*, *which project needs me?*, or *what are my running sessions doing right now?* Switching views never changes your sessions; it only changes how the session area is laid out.

## Switch views

The **layout mode switcher** is the small icon next to *Session List* at the left end of the session toolbar. The icon always shows the view you are in now.

![Choose a view drawer with a live preview of Command View on top and the five views listed below](../../img/user-guide/views/01-layout-switcher.png)

1. Click the switcher. The **Choose a view** drawer opens on the left.
2. Point at a view, or move through the list with the arrow keys (`↑` `↓` `←` `→`, `Home`, `End`), to preview it. The stage at the top assembles a miniature of that view and explains **Best for**, **You'll see**, and **Trade-off**.
3. Click a view to switch right away. The drawer closes, and the active view carries a check mark and the label *Current*.

HagiCode remembers your choice in this browser and restores it the next time you open the workbench.

:::note[Narrow windows use one list]
Below 1024 px wide, which includes tablets and phones, HagiCode shows a single session list and hides the switcher, and sessions open in a drawer. Your chosen view comes back as soon as the window is wide enough again.
:::

## The five views at a glance

| View | Badge | Best for | Trade-off |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Classic** | Focused | Working through one session at a time, with its conversation open beside the list. | Only one session is in focus, so the overall flow is harder to see. |
| **Execution Flow Kanban** | Flow | Following work from queue to done and acting from the operations panel. | Uses the most room: swimlanes plus the right-side panel. |
| **Dashboard Kanban** | Compact | Scanning every session's status when you don't need to follow the proposal stages. | Lanes group sessions by status, so proposal stages are not separated. |
| **Project × Status Matrix** | Matrix | Spotting which project needs you across many projects at once. | Summarises sessions as counts, so a single session is one step further. |
| **Command View** | Command | Watching several running sessions and answering their questions without leaving the wall. | Keeps several sessions live at once, so it suits a large screen. |

## Classic

![Classic view with the session list on the left and the conversation of the selected session on the right](../../img/user-guide/views/02-classic.png)

Classic is the three-column layout: the **project rail**, a compact **session list**, and a **detail column**. Click a session and its detail opens in the third column with the **Info**, **Conversation**, and **Review** tabs and the message box, so you can read and reply without leaving the page. Click a project in the rail to see the project's details in the same column. The arrow at the top of the list collapses it to give the conversation more room.

Choose Classic when you work on one session at a time. It is the only view that needs a window at least 1024 px wide and keeps the conversation permanently on screen; in the four board views a session opens in a drawer over the board.

## Execution Flow Kanban

![Execution Flow Kanban with Proposal & Review, Execution, and Archived lanes and the project panel on the right](../../img/user-guide/views/03-execution-flow-kanban.png)

Execution Flow Kanban follows a session from idea to finish. It has three lanes:

| Lane | Sessions in it |
| --- | --- |
| **Proposal & Review** | Ideas being optimized, drafted, written up, or reviewed. |
| **Execution** | Sessions that are executing, waiting for your reply, or have finished executing. |
| **Archived** | Sessions that are archiving, completed, or archived. |

Each card shows the session number, project, type icon, title, last activity, and a status badge. Click a card to open the session in a drawer. The panel on the right is the project panel: it summarizes all your projects, and when you select a project in the rail it shows that project's quick actions and repository status. You can change or hide this panel in *Settings → Process & Interaction → Kanban Right Panel*; see [Settings](/en-US/user-guide/settings).

This is the view used throughout the rest of this guide.

## Dashboard Kanban

![Dashboard Kanban with Pending, In Progress, and Archived lanes](../../img/user-guide/views/04-dashboard-kanban.png)

Dashboard Kanban uses the same cards but groups them by **status** instead of by workflow stage:

| Lane | Sessions in it |
| --- | --- |
| **Pending** | Sessions waiting for you: new, drafted, in review, waiting for a reply, or finished executing. |
| **In Progress** | Sessions the Agent is working on right now. |
| **Archived** | Sessions that are finished or archived. |

Use it when you only want to know what needs attention, what is running, and what is done, and you don't need the proposal stages split out. A session that asks you a question lands in **Pending**, so that lane is your to-do list.

## Project × Status Matrix

![Project × Status Matrix with one row per project and Pending, In Progress, and Archived columns](../../img/user-guide/views/05-matrix.png)

The matrix puts **projects in rows** and the Dashboard Kanban lanes — **Pending**, **In Progress**, **Archived** — in columns. Each cell shows how many sessions the project has in that lane and the status that is most urgent. The project column carries the project name, its avatar, a **Needs your attention** count (sessions waiting for your reply or that look stalled), and the buttons to start a new session.

![The Drafted cell of orbit-notes expanded, with its three session cards below the row](../../img/user-guide/views/06-matrix-expanded.png)

- Click a cell that holds **one** session to open it.
- Click a cell that holds **several** sessions to expand their cards below that row. Click the cell again to collapse them.
- Move between cells with the arrow keys.
- Select a single project in the rail to show only its row; **View all projects** returns to the full grid.

The matrix is the quickest way to answer "which project needs me?" when you work across many projects.

## Command View

![Command View with the session sidebar on the left, a wall of six live tiles, and the project panel on the right](../../img/user-guide/views/07-command-view.png)

Command View is a control wall for sessions that are running. It has three parts:

**Session sidebar** (left) groups every session:

| Group | Sessions in it |
| --- | --- |
| **Needs You** | Sessions waiting for your reply, and running sessions that have been silent for a long time. |
| **Running** | Sessions the Agent is working on. |
| **Other** | Everything else that is not archived. |
| **Archived** | Archived sessions. |

Click a row to put that session on the wall and open it in the drawer, or click its pin button to put it on the wall without opening it.

**Wall** (middle) is a grid of **live tiles**. Each tile shows the session title, project, status, quick action buttons, the newest few entries of the conversation, and how many seconds ago the session last did something. When an Agent asks a question, the question card appears inside the tile so you can answer it there. Click a tile to open the full session in the drawer, or click the **×** on a tile to take it off the wall.

The **Tiles** selector above the wall sets its size: **4**, **6**, **8**, **10**, or **12** tiles, or **Auto**, which sizes the wall to your sessions. When you open Command View, HagiCode fills the wall with your unarchived sessions in priority order, up to 12: sessions waiting for you first, then running ones, then the rest. After that the wall stays as you arrange it: pin a session to add it, or remove one with the **×**. Choosing **Auto** hands the wall back to HagiCode, which then keeps it filled from your sessions automatically.

**Project panel** (right) is the same panel the other board views show.

Choose Command View when several Agents run at once and you mostly need to notice and answer them. Because it keeps several sessions live, it works best on a large screen.

## Which view should I use?

- **One conversation at a time** → Classic.
- **Following each task from proposal to done** → Execution Flow Kanban.
- **A quick status check** → Dashboard Kanban.
- **Many projects, and you want to know which one needs you** → Project × Status Matrix.
- **Several Agents running, and you want to answer them as they ask** → Command View.

You can switch at any time; nothing is lost. Many people keep Execution Flow Kanban as a default and open Command View while several sessions run.
